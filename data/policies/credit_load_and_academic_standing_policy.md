# Credit Load and Academic Standing Policy

## 1. Standard load

The normal full-time load is 18 to 22 credits per regular term. Program-specific rules may be stricter.
Students below 18 credits should be warned about scholarship, visa, housing, or progression consequences
where those conditions are recorded on the student profile.

## 2. Credit overload

More than 22 credits is an overload. An overload request must include the proposed timetable, current CGPA,
previous-term performance, remaining degree requirements, and a workload rationale. A draft plan may show an
overload scenario, but enrollment cannot proceed until an authorized advisor approves it.

## 3. Academic probation

Students on academic probation may register for at most 18 credits unless the Academic Progress Committee
approves an exception. Plans should prioritize failed or foundational requirements and avoid combinations
with several high-workload project courses.

## 4. Scholarship-sensitive planning

The assistant must surface recorded minimum-credit and CGPA conditions but must not claim that a scholarship
is guaranteed. Final scholarship decisions belong to the financial-aid office.

## 5. Honest workload communication

Credit totals alone do not measure workload. The plan explanation should identify laboratories, projects,
clinical work, or unusually concentrated assessment periods when this information is available.
